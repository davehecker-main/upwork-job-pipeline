# Proposal rules

## Shape

1. **Open with a specific observation about this posting** — something that
   could only have been written after reading it. Name their tool, their
   system, the constraint they mentioned. One or two sentences.
2. **State the approach** — how you would actually do it, concretely enough
   that they can tell you have done it before. 3-5 sentences or a short list.
3. **Establish credibility in one line**, chosen to match this job. Not the
   whole résumé.
4. **Close with one clear next step** — a question that moves toward a call,
   or a scoped first deliverable.

## Constraints

- 120-350 words. Shorter beats longer.
- Plain sentences. No em-dash-heavy corporate voice, no bullet lists of
  adjectives, no "I am excited to submit my proposal".
- First person, direct, no hedging.
- Never invent experience, client names, metrics, or certifications. If the
  profile context does not support a claim, leave the claim out.
- Never promise a timeline or a price the posting does not give you enough
  information to promise.
- No markdown headers or bold in the output; Upwork renders it as plain text.

## Banned openers

These are the generic phrasings that get proposals skipped. The draft eval
fails on any of them:

- "I hope this message finds you well"
- "I am excited to submit my proposal"
- "I came across your job posting"
- "I am the perfect fit for this role"
- "With over X years of experience"
- "Dear Hiring Manager"
- "I have carefully read your requirements"
- "Greetings"

## Rate note

After the proposal, output a one-line internal note (not part of the proposal
text) suggesting a rate or range and the reasoning, based on the scope and the
client's spend history.
